### The town our engineers work from

Our office is at Caxton Point Business Centre in Stevenage, so this is the one location where being nearby is a fact rather than a claim. The town’s employment base is also unusually specialised for its size, which shapes the work.
- [CTA] 01Aerospace and defence engineering sets the tone on process
The engineering employers in the town work to documented process and controlled change, and their suppliers are expected to do the same. Support that cannot evidence what changed and when is a poor fit for that supply chain.
- [CTA] 02A cell and gene therapy cluster on the doorstep
The bioscience campus has drawn a concentration of laboratory and life-science businesses, where instrument PCs, data integrity and validated configurations mean patching is a planned activity rather than an automatic one.
- [CTA] 03Industrial estates with practical, physical constraints
Around Caxton Way and the surrounding estates, the recurring issues are cabling in older units, coverage across workshop space and connectivity options that are more limited than the postcode suggests. Being local means we can look rather than guess.
- [CTA] 04Same-day attendance because we are in the town
Our engineers work from Caxton Point Business Centre, so for Stevenage businesses onsite attendance is a matter of crossing the town rather than planning a journey. Where equipment cannot be diagnosed remotely, that is the practical difference between a morning’s disruption and a lost day.
- [CTA] 05Town-centre regeneration and a shifting commercial map
Long-running regeneration has moved a good deal of Stevenage’s commercial activity between the town centre and the surrounding estates. Businesses relocating locally still face full connectivity lead times, which is the detail most often underestimated in a short move.
- [CTA] 06Defence and aerospace work brings questions about where data sits
Supplying into that sector means being asked where information is stored and processed, who administers the systems holding it, and how access by anyone outside a defined group is prevented. Those questions are answerable, but only if the decisions were made deliberately: choosing data residency when a tenant is set up, keeping administrative access tightly held and logged, and being able to show the arrangement rather than describe it.
- [CTA] 07Controlled environments limit what can be touched and when
Cleanrooms and laboratories restrict physical access, and equipment inside them frequently cannot be interrupted mid-process. That changes how support works: as much remote diagnosis as possible, physical work batched into planned access rather than done reactively, and a clear understanding of which systems have runs that must not be disturbed. Knowing that in advance avoids the situation where an engineer is on site and cannot enter the room.
