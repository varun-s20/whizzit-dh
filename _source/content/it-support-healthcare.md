### Clinical systems, patient data, and no convenient downtime

Healthcare providers combine special-category personal data with services that cannot simply pause. A clinic whose systems are unavailable is not inconvenienced; it is cancelling appointments and turning patients away, which makes availability a clinical concern rather than an operational one.
- [CTA] 01Access control that reflects clinical roles
Who may see a patient record is determined by role and by legitimate relationship, not by convenience. Implementing that properly means role-based permissions, meaningful audit logging and periodic review, so that access can be explained rather than assumed.
- [CTA] 02Clinical and practice systems set the constraints
Patient management, imaging and referral systems often have specific requirements for browsers, endpoints and connectivity, and they are rarely flexible. Support work here is mostly about keeping a defined environment stable rather than modernising it for its own sake.
- [CTA] 03Recovery time is the number that matters
For a provider running clinics, the meaningful question is not whether a backup exists but how quickly a working system returns. Defining an acceptable recovery time, then testing against it, is the part that turns a backup into continuity.
