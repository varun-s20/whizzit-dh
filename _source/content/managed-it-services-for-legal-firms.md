### What makes IT different in a law firm

A practice runs on confidentiality, an audit trail and files that have to survive for decades. That combination puts different demands on IT than almost any other professional service, and it is where most generic support arrangements fall short.
- [CTA] 01Payment redirection fraud targets conveyancers directly
Interception of completion emails and last-minute changes of bank details is one of the most common frauds aimed at law firms, and it arrives through the mailbox rather than the firewall. The controls that matter are email authentication, impersonation protection, tight rules on who can change payment information, and training that teaches fee earners to verify by phone against a known number.
- [CTA] 02Retention measured in decades, not years
Wills, deeds and matter files often have to be produced long after the matter closed and long after the software that created them was retired. That is an archiving question rather than a backup question: readable formats, documented restore paths, and storage you can still search in fifteen years.
- [CTA] 03Case and practice management sits at the centre
Time recording, matter files and the client account all live in the practice management system, so its availability effectively sets the firm’s working hours. We manage the platforms and infrastructure around it, keep the integrations and access working, and treat downtime there as a first-priority incident.
