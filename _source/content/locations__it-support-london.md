### How to choose an IT support company in London

Most London providers describe themselves in similar terms, which makes them hard to tell apart. These are the five questions worth asking anyone you shortlist, including us.
- [CTA] 01Ask what “fast response” actually means
A response commitment is only meaningful if it is stated in hours or working days and applies to a defined priority level. Ask for the priority framework and the committed response time at each level, and ask what proportion of issues are resolved on first contact.
- [CTA] 02Ask where the engineers are actually based
A London address does not always mean staffed premises in London, and plenty of providers list a registered or serviced office. Ask where engineers physically work from and how onsite visits are arranged. Our registered address is on City Road and our engineers work from Caxton Point Business Centre in Stevenage, travelling into London for onsite work.
- [CTA] 03Check the accreditations yourself
Certifications are checkable, so check them. Cyber Essentials certificates are listed on the IASME certified organisation register, and Microsoft partner status can be confirmed through Microsoft. Be wary of unverifiable claims such as “award-winning” with no named award, or ISO certification that is stated but not evidenced.
- [CTA] 04Ask who you will actually speak to
For a business of five to a hundred and thirty people, the difference between a named team who know your setup and a rotating first-line queue shows up the first time something breaks. Ask how many people will handle your account and whether the service desk is UK-based.
- [CTA] 05Ask what leaving looks like
Before signing, ask what notice you need to give, who owns your documentation and licences, and what handover you would receive if you moved on. A provider confident in the service will answer this without hesitation.
