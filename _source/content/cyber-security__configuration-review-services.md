### Small configuration decisions can have long-term consequences

Technology settings are often changed little by little as businesses evolve. A new employee needs additional access, a cloud service is introduced, security policies are adjusted or a supplier makes changes during a project. Individually these updates seem minor, but over time they can leave systems configured in ways that no longer reflect how the business operates.
Configuration issues rarely appear after a single change. More often, they develop after years of updates made by different IT providers, software vendors and internal teams. Everything continues working, yet the organisation is carrying unnecessary operational and cyber security risk without realising it.
Reviews examine how your systems are actually configured against how they were intended to be, and the output is a prioritised remediation list rather than a raw scan dump. Across 2025 our average first response time was 9 minutes and our average resolution time was 6 hours, measured across every ticket we handled.
Common configuration risks we find
- User permissions that have expanded over time without review
- Security features available but not fully configured
- Microsoft 365 settings that no longer reflect current working practices
- Cloud services configured differently across departments
- Legacy settings left behind after previous technology projects
- Device policies applied inconsistently across the organisation
