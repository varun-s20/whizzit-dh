Before anything moves we find out what actually exists. #### Build and pilot

We set up the new tenant properly, with security baselines, multi-factor authentication and licensing sorted from the start rather than retrofitted later. Then a small pilot group moves first. It is far better to find the awkward bits with four willing people than with all fifty at once.

#### Bulk move and cutover

Mail and files copy across in the background over several days while everyone carries on working in the old system. The switch itself is the short part, scheduled deliberately for a quiet window, followed by a few days of us being close by while people find the things only real use uncovers.
Seven days is the configuration effort, not the calendar. A 50 user move typically spans two to three weeks end to end, because the bulk copy runs in the background and the pilot needs a few days of real use before the rest follow.

### Where migrations go wrong

Moving mailboxes is the easy bit. Almost every migration that goes badly goes badly for the same handful of reasons, and none of them are the mail.
- [CTA] The things nobody owns
Shared mailboxes, distribution lists and meeting rooms get forgotten because no single person owns them, then reappear the morning after cutover when nobody can book anything.
- [CTA] Permissions and connected apps
Permissions on shared folders are assumed to carry over neatly and rarely do, so somebody either loses access to a folder they need or quietly gains access to one they should not have. Third party applications that authenticate against the old system stop working until each one is reconnected, and the list of them is always longer than anyone expects.
- [CTA] Phones, and the licences still billing
Everyone remembers the laptops. Half the workforce has mail on a phone that nobody has recorded anywhere, and if that is not handled they spend cutover morning locked out and irritated. Old licences are the last one, still quietly billing months later because nobody cancelled them.
- [CTA] None of it is difficult
It just has to be written down beforehand and worked through, which is precisely what the discovery stage is for.
Caught during discovery
- Shared mailboxes, distribution lists and meeting rooms
- Folder permissions and who really needs what
- Applications signing in with the old accounts
- Mobile devices with mail on them
- Mailbox and file sizes against your new licences
- Old subscriptions to cancel after go live

### The move we get asked about most

Google to Microsoft 365 has its own quirks, and they are worth knowing before you start rather than after. Google Docs, Sheets and Slides are not Office files. They convert on the way across, and anything with heavy formatting or a script behind it needs checking rather than assuming. Gmail labels are not folders either. A message can carry three labels at once, a folder structure cannot hold that idea, so decisions have to be made about what the mailbox looks like afterwards.
- [CTA] Drives & calendars
- [CTA] Connected apps
- [CTA] Done for real
Shared Drives map onto SharePoint and Teams, which is usually an improvement, but it is a genuine restructure rather than a copy and paste. Calendar resources such as meeting rooms need rebuilding.
Any app that people sign into with their Google account has to be pointed at Microsoft instead, and that includes the ones nobody mentioned in the kick off meeting.
Ixia Clinical came across from Google Workspace to Microsoft 365 with ten users and more than a terabyte of email and files, without disruption to clinical work.
