### What we account for in Birmingham

Birmingham pairs a dense professional-services core around Colmore Row with a manufacturing and engineering supply chain spread across the wider West Midlands. Those two halves of the city want very different things from IT, and a business often sits in both at once.
- [CTA] 01City-centre professional firms, multi-floor and multi-tenant
Serviced and multi-tenant buildings in the business district rarely let you touch the incoming connection or the risers. That pushes the design towards resilient wireless, cellular failover and cloud-first services rather than anything that assumes control of the building’s infrastructure.
- [CTA] 02Supply-chain security requirements arrive from customers
Firms supplying automotive and aerospace primes increasingly find Cyber Essentials or an equivalent written into purchase terms. The certification itself is straightforward; the awkward part is usually the unsupported machine or shared login that has to be dealt with first.
- [CTA] 03Sites across the conurbation, not one address
Operations often run across Birmingham, Solihull and the Black Country, which makes consistency the real problem: the same policies, the same patch state and the same support route at every location, rather than three arrangements that grew separately.
- [CTA] 04Within reasonable reach for onsite work
Birmingham is one of the closer locations to our Stevenage base, so where hardware genuinely needs attention a same-working-day visit is a realistic commitment rather than a hopeful one. For manufacturers in the wider West Midlands, that is often the deciding factor.
- [CTA] 05Sustained construction and relocation activity
Large-scale infrastructure and development work across the city has kept commercial relocation unusually frequent. Moves are where IT most often goes wrong, and the recurring cause is connectivity lead times booked too late rather than anything technical.
- [CTA] 06Several sites should still mean one identity
Operating across the conurbation often means each location grew its own arrangements, with separate logins, separate file stores and separate ways of doing things. Consolidating to a single identity per person, with access granted by role, removes most of the friction people feel when they move between sites and removes the accounts that accumulate when someone changes location. It also makes leavers a single action rather than a checklist per building.
- [CTA] 07Project sites open and close, and IT has to match
Where work runs on a project basis, sites appear for a period and then stand down. Treating connectivity, hardware and access as something to set up and recover repeatedly, with a standard build rather than a bespoke one each time, keeps mobilisation quick and stops equipment quietly disappearing at the end of a job. The cost of a standard kit list is recovered the second time it is used.
