### Our own county, and what that changes

Hertfordshire is where our office actually is, at Caxton Point Business Centre in Stevenage. That makes it the one area where an engineer genuinely is nearby rather than travelling, and it shapes what we can commit to across the county.
- [CTA] 01Film, television and studio production in the south of the county
The studio complexes clustered around south Hertfordshire generate production workloads with the storage and file-transfer demands that come with them, often on short project timescales with temporary crew needing access and then losing it.
- [CTA] 02A life sciences corridor with its own requirements
The cell and gene therapy cluster centred on Stevenage brings laboratory instrumentation, data retention expectations and validated environments where a routine update is not a routine decision. Change control matters more here than speed.
- [CTA] 03Commuter-belt professional firms working three ways
Many Hertfordshire businesses split their week between a local office, London client sites and home. That makes identity and device security the centre of the design, because the office perimeter is not where the work happens.
- [CTA] 04The one county where nearby is literal
Because the office is at Caxton Point in Stevenage, Hertfordshire is the only area where an engineer is genuinely local rather than travelling. Same-working-day attendance is a straightforward commitment here, and for laboratory or production equipment that cannot be diagnosed remotely, that proximity is the substantive difference.
- [CTA] 05The A1(M) corridor brings warehousing and distribution
Alongside the professional and life-science base, the corridor through the county carries a substantial amount of warehousing and logistics. Those sites need coverage across large open spaces and scanning equipment that stays connected while moving, which is a survey-led design rather than an office one.
- [CTA] 06Freelance and short-contract access needs an expiry date
Production and life sciences work both bring people in for defined periods. Accounts created for a project have a habit of remaining active long after it ends, because nothing prompts anyone to remove them. Issuing access with an end date set at the point of creation, rather than relying on somebody remembering, keeps the list of who can reach your systems close to the list of who should. It also makes the offboarding conversation trivial instead of archaeological.
- [CTA] 07Validated and instrument-attached systems change on their own schedule
Lab environments frequently include software tied to an instrument, or a configuration that has been formally validated. Those cannot simply follow the same patching cadence as everything else, because a change may require revalidation. The workable approach is to treat them as a separate group with their own agreed schedule and compensating controls, rather than either patching them blindly or quietly excluding them and forgetting they exist.
