### Two lines are not resilience if they share a duct

Buying a second connection feels like buying resilience, and often is not. If both circuits run from the same exchange, along the same route, or terminate on the same piece of your equipment, one contractor with a digger takes out both. Diversity has to be designed rather than assumed.
- [CTA] 01Diverse carrier, diverse route, diverse hardware
Genuine resilience means the two services do not share a failure point: different providers where possible, physically different routes into the building, and separate terminating equipment. Where a truly diverse fixed route is not available at a site, 4G or 5G backup gives you a path that fails independently by definition, because it does not use the ground at all.
- [CTA] 02Failover that happens before anyone notices
The value is in how fast and how quietly the switch happens. A failover that drops every call and every session is better than an outage, but only just. SD-WAN monitors the health of each path continuously and moves traffic when a link degrades, not only when it dies completely, which matters because slow and lossy is more common than fully down.
- [CTA] 03Not all traffic deserves the same treatment
A voice call is ruined by 100ms of jitter that a file upload would never notice. Policy-based routing lets you put real-time traffic on the most stable path and bulk transfers on the cheaper one, and change that automatically when conditions shift. This is the part that a straight second line cannot do at all.
- [CTA] 04Bandwidth is rarely the actual problem
Most sites that feel slow are not short of bandwidth. They are suffering from contention at peak times, a saturated upload path, or one backup job running in business hours. Measuring before upgrading usually costs less than upgrading, and sometimes shows the circuit was never the constraint.
