### What we account for in Manchester

Greater Manchester combines a professional-services core, a well-established media and broadcast cluster, and one of the larger digital and tech scenes in the UK. Each brings a different bottleneck, and they rarely overlap.
- [CTA] 01Media work is a storage and bandwidth problem first
Production files are large, versioned and edited collaboratively, which strains both the connection and the backup window. Getting the storage tiering and sync rules right matters more here than raw device specification.
- [CTA] 02Fast-growing teams outgrow their IT decisions quickly
A structure that suited twelve people becomes the constraint at forty, usually showing up as shared logins and permissions nobody can explain. Building identity and access properly early is far cheaper than untangling it later.
- [CTA] 03A city region, not a single city
Businesses commonly operate across Manchester, Salford, Stockport and Trafford. The aim is one consistent standard across all of them rather than separate arrangements that diverge over time.
- [CTA] 04Onsite work planned, remote work the norm
Our engineers travel from London and Stevenage, so Manchester visits are arranged rather than same-day. For the professional, digital and media businesses that make up much of the city’s base, this is rarely the constraint people expect, because the work is cloud-hosted and the faults are resolvable remotely.
- [CTA] 05Co-working and serviced space is unusually common
A large share of Manchester’s smaller businesses sit in shared or serviced buildings where the network belongs to the landlord. That removes several options entirely and makes device-level security, identity controls and cellular failover more important than anything that assumes control of the router.
- [CTA] 06Short-term collaborators need scoped access, not a full account
Agency, production and digital work runs on people who are involved for one project. Giving each of them a full account with broad access is quick and creates a long tail of dormant logins. Guest access scoped to a single workspace or folder, with an expiry, covers almost every case and leaves nothing behind when the work finishes. It is also far easier to answer a client question about who could see their material.
- [CTA] 07Archive and backup solve different problems
Finished work that must be retrievable in three years is an archive question. Recovering yesterday’s state after something went wrong is a backup question. Using one for the other is where costs and disappointment come from, either paying premium storage to hold completed projects indefinitely, or discovering the backup retention window closed long before anyone came looking. Deciding which material is which, once, makes both cheaper and more reliable.
