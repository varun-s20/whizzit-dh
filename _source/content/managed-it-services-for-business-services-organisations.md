### Many clients, one estate, and confidentiality between them

Firms that work inside other people’s businesses carry a particular obligation: information from one client must not become visible to another, even accidentally, and even when the same consultant works on both. That is a structural requirement rather than a policy statement.
- [CTA] 01Separation by design, not by good intentions
Per-client workspaces, permissions that expire when an engagement ends, and a clear rule for where client material is allowed to live. The alternative, a shared drive that nobody prunes, is where confidentiality quietly breaks down.
- [CTA] 02Working on the client’s site and the client’s network
Consultants routinely connect to guest wireless, use client systems and move between environments in a week. Devices need to be secure enough to be trusted anywhere, which puts the emphasis on the endpoint and on identity rather than on the office perimeter.
- [CTA] 03Projects start and stop, so the setup has to as well
Onboarding and offboarding are constant rather than occasional, and each one is an access decision. Automating joiners and leavers is the difference between a licence bill that reflects reality and one that only grows.
