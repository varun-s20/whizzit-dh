### What we account for across Wales

Wales is a coverage question as much as a technical one. The distance between Cardiff, Swansea, mid Wales and the north is considerable, connectivity is uneven outside the urban areas, and there is a language requirement that does not apply elsewhere in our coverage.
- [CTA] 01Connectivity cannot be assumed outside the cities
Fixed-line options in rural and valley locations are frequently limited, and mobile coverage varies within a few miles. Designing for the connection that exists, with cellular failover and offline-tolerant working where needed, beats specifying for the connection you wish were there.
- [CTA] 02Welsh language obligations reach into everyday systems
Organisations providing public-facing services may need to offer Welsh alongside English, which affects templates, forms, signatures and locale settings across the estate. Building it in from the start avoids a retrofit.
- [CTA] 03Supplying the public sector brings its requirements with it
A large share of Welsh business touches the public sector somewhere, whether that is a health board, a local authority or a Welsh Government programme. Those relationships tend to pass their assurance expectations down the chain, so the questions arrive in a procurement pack rather than from your own risk register: how access is controlled, where data is held, what your recovery position is, and whether you hold a recognised certification. Being able to answer without a scramble is usually the difference between staying on a framework and quietly dropping off it.
- [CTA] 04Onsite attendance across Wales is planned work
Because our engineers travel from London and Stevenage, a visit to west or north Wales is scheduled rather than same-day. We would rather set that expectation at the outset than imply a call-out radius we cannot honour. It also changes how we specify: more emphasis on remote diagnostics, on hardware that is unlikely to need physical attention, and on spare capacity held locally where a failure would stop the business.
- [CTA] 05Seasonal and dispersed operating patterns
Tourism, hospitality and agriculture give parts of Wales a pronounced seasonal shape, with headcount and transaction volumes that swing through the year. Licensing and capacity that suit August are wasteful in February, and the reverse causes outages. Reviewing that annually rather than renewing on autopilot usually saves more than any single technical change.
- [CTA] 06Sites with unreliable connectivity need to keep working offline
Where a connection is intermittent rather than slow, the design question changes from bandwidth to tolerance. Systems that assume constant connectivity will fail badly on a poor line, whereas ones that hold work locally and reconcile later will not. Choosing software that behaves sensibly offline, and keeping a mobile fallback that engages automatically, tends to matter more at a rural site than any upgrade to the primary line.
- [CTA] 07Seasonal hiring is an identity problem in disguise
Businesses that scale up for a season need dozens of people productive quickly and gone cleanly afterwards. Done informally that means shared logins and accounts nobody closes. A prepared template for a seasonal role, created on arrival and expiring at the end of the contract, takes the same effort the first time and almost none thereafter, while leaving a clear record of who had access and when.
