Cardiff in particular

What we account for in Cardiff

Cardiff carries a mix most cities do not: a substantial public-sector and public-adjacent economy, a media production cluster, and a financial services presence. It also brings a genuine technical requirement almost nowhere else in our coverage does.

01 / 07

Welsh language is a configuration requirement, not a preference

Organisations delivering public-facing services in Wales may have obligations to offer Welsh alongside English. In IT terms that reaches into templates, signatures, forms, website content and sometimes keyboard and locale settings. It is far easier to build in at setup than to retrofit across a live estate.

02 / 07

Public-sector adjacency raises the assurance bar

Suppliers to Welsh public bodies are often asked to evidence security controls in a formal questionnaire rather than a conversation. Having documented answers on access control, retention and incident response ready in advance saves weeks on a tender.

03 / 07

Media production moves large files on deadlines

Video and audio work generates volumes that ordinary office broadband and ordinary backup windows were not sized for. The practical answers are staged local storage, sensible sync rules and being honest about what a connection can actually carry.

04 / 07

Onsite attendance is scheduled, and we say so

Engineers travel from London and Stevenage, so a Cardiff visit is planned rather than immediate. We would rather state that plainly than list a virtual Cardiff address and imply otherwise. Remote resolution handles most of what arises, and we plan onsite work around genuine need.

05 / 07

Operating either side of the border

Businesses based in Cardiff frequently trade across England and Wales, which can mean two sets of public-sector frameworks, procurement portals and language expectations to satisfy from a single IT estate. Designing for both at the outset avoids maintaining two half-solutions.

06 / 07

Broadcast and production deadlines narrow the change window

When output is scheduled, maintenance cannot simply happen on a convenient evening. Work has to fit around transmission and delivery commitments, which means agreeing windows in advance and having a tested way back if a change misbehaves. The alternative, deferring everything until it is urgent, is how organisations end up doing risky work at the worst possible moment.

07 / 07

Contributors who are not employees still need access

Production work brings in freelancers, contributors and post houses who need particular material for a defined period. Handling that through named, time-limited access to specific content, rather than broad shares or files passed around, keeps control of the material and makes it possible to say precisely who could reach what. It is also markedly easier than trying to recall a link that has already been forwarded.

Managed IT in Cardiff: common questions

Not covered here? Ask us directly — same working day response.

Do you have an office in Cardiff?

No. Our offices are at 124 City Rd in London and Caxton Point in Stevenage, and we are straight about that rather than listing a virtual address. Cardiff is covered by our UK service desk plus a team of field engineers who travel to customer sites. In practice most issues never need a visit at all: we respond the same working day and resolve most issues remotely. When a site visit is requested, we aim to have an engineer with you the same working day.

We handle sensitive client data. How do you approach security?

We hold Cyber Essentials certification ourselves and can take you through it, which matters because your provider is part of your attack surface. Day to day it means enforced multi-factor authentication, managed endpoint protection, email security, patching on a schedule and backups that are tested by restoring from them.

Can you support hybrid teams working across South Wales?

Yes, and it is the norm now rather than the exception. Support follows the person rather than the desk: devices are managed and monitored wherever they are, access is secured with multi-factor authentication, and the service desk does not care which building someone is sitting in.

Do you work with financial and professional services firms?

Yes. What those businesses tend to need is unglamorous and non-negotiable: email that always works, files available and version-controlled, tight access control and backups that can be proven. We are a Microsoft Cloud Solution Provider, so Microsoft 365 and Azure are handled directly rather than through a reseller chain.

What does onboarding involve?

We start with an assessment of your current systems and what the business actually needs, agree a support plan, deploy the tooling we use for remote assistance, monitoring and patching, then go live. You get a named point of contact rather than a ticket portal and nothing else.
