Leeds in particular

What we account for in Leeds

Leeds has one of the largest concentrations of financial, legal and professional services outside London. That density changes the IT conversation, because a majority of the businesses we would support there operate inside somebody’s regulatory perimeter.

01 / 07

Regulated firms have to evidence, not just assert

A law firm answering to the SRA and a financial firm answering to the FCA both need to show how access is controlled, how records are retained and what happens during an incident. Documentation is part of the service in that context, not an optional extra.

02 / 07

Client money and client data raise the stakes on email

Payment redirection fraud targets exactly this mix of sectors, and it arrives by email rather than through the network. Authentication, impersonation protection and a firm rule about verifying bank details by telephone do more good here than additional perimeter spending.

03 / 07

City-centre growth means frequent moves and fit-outs

Firms in Leeds expand into adjacent floors and relocate more often than average. Planning the network and connectivity lead times early is what keeps a move from becoming a fortnight of tethered laptops.

04 / 07

Onsite attendance from the South East, planned in advance

Engineers travel from London and Stevenage, so a Leeds visit is scheduled work. Given how much of the city’s business base is desk-and-cloud rather than machinery, that matters less here than in a manufacturing town, and remote resolution covers the large majority of what arises.

05 / 07

A health and public-sector technology cluster

Leeds has an established concentration of health-related and public-sector digital organisations alongside its financial and legal core. Suppliers into that ecosystem are routinely asked to evidence security controls and data handling formally, which makes documentation part of the deliverable rather than an afterthought.

06 / 07

Retention is a records decision before it is a storage one

Regulated firms carry obligations about how long client records are kept and how they are produced if asked. That is a policy question, but it lands as a technology one: retention rules on mailboxes and document libraries, a legal hold that survives an employee leaving, and a search that can actually find everything on a matter. Setting it deliberately is straightforward. Reconstructing it later, from mailboxes people have tidied and archives nobody documented, is the expensive version.

07 / 07

Sending client documents by attachment is the habit worth breaking

The safest change most professional firms can make is to stop moving sensitive documents as email attachments. A portal or a secure share means access can be withdrawn, viewing is recorded, and a message sent to the wrong recipient does not hand over the file. It also removes the temptation to protect a document with a password sent in a second email, which is not protection at all if both messages reach the same inbox.

IT support in Leeds: frequently asked questions

Not covered here? Ask us directly — same working day response.

Do you have an office in Leeds?

No. Our offices are at 124 City Rd in London and Caxton Point in Stevenage, and we would rather say so than list a virtual address in Leeds. The city is covered by our UK service desk plus field engineers who travel to customer sites. Most issues never need a visit: we respond the same working day and resolve most issues remotely. When a visit is requested we aim to have an engineer with you the same working day.

You support a financial services firm in Leeds. Do you work with regulated businesses?

We do support financial services firms, and what they need is usually unglamorous rather than exotic: tight access control, enforced multi-factor authentication, email that is properly secured, and backups that have been tested by actually restoring from them. We are Cyber Essentials Certified ourselves. We are an IT provider rather than a compliance consultancy, so we support your obligations rather than signing them off.

Can you help with backup and business continuity?

Yes, and it is worth separating the two. Backup is having a copy; continuity is knowing how quickly you could actually be working again, and from what. We cover Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace backup as well as wider continuity and disaster recovery planning.

How are urgent issues handled?

Faults are graded P1 to P5, each with its own SLA. P1 means the business cannot operate, a total outage or a dead server, and gets immediate attention. Everything below still carries a committed response time, so lower-priority tickets are not left to drift.

Can you work alongside our internal IT?

Yes, and it is a common arrangement. We can take the day-to-day tickets, monitoring and patching so your internal person concentrates on projects and the things only they can do, or we can sit behind them as escalation and holiday cover only.
